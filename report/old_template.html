How sure are we?

How to read this

Each strategy starts from a sceptical belief: racing markets are close to efficient, so the true long-run return is probably near 0% and rarely beyond ±5%. Every settled bet then updates that belief. Best guess is the observed return pulled toward zero, more strongly when there are few bets or the results swing a lot (big-priced winners). Chance the edge is real is the probability the true return is above zero.

Use it for staking: keep stakes small while the chance sits between 30% and 80%; only increase when it stays above about 90% on live bets. One big winner moves the observed return a lot but the best guess only a little, which is the point.

Winners vs expected (xW)

For each list: how many winners it produced against how many the market expected. Expected winners (xW) adds up each pick's chance from the 9am prices with the bookmakers' margin removed, and from Betfair's starting price (BSP) where it is in. Actual ÷ expected above 1.00 means more winners than the market expected. It says nothing about profit on its own. Beating the 9am prices is easier than beating BSP.

How to read the luck check
Winners swing a lot by chance. The luck check measures how far the actual count is from the expected count, allowing for the normal day-to-day swing. "Ahead beyond luck" or "Behind beyond luck" needs the gap to be about twice the normal swing; everything else is "Within luck". A list needs a few hundred picks before a real edge would usually show up as beyond luck.

Angles (paper test)

About these angles

Two angles passed a sealed test on six years of GB Flat racing (42 angles tested, discovered on 2020–23, confirmed on 2024–26, judged against Betfair SP). Both beat BSP in 6 of 7 years:

Sprint after a mile: running at 6f or less after a last run at a mile or more. Actual/expected vs BSP 1.19 and 1.20; +11% at BSP up to 20/1.
First time on soft: soft or heavy ground today, never run on it before, at least 3 runs. Actual/expected 1.08 and 1.06; +4% at BSP up to 20/1.

Lay: up in trip after fading (added 16 Oct 2026): stepping up 1f or more after a last run where the horse's finishing speed was in the bottom quarter of its race. Horses like this won only 0.84 and 0.92 times what BSP implied in 2023–24 and 2025–26. Paper-tracked as a lay at BSP capped at 10.0, £1 liability; a winner costs £1. This rule was found by looking at the data, so only the forward record counts.

With 42 angles tested, about one could pass by luck, so these are paper-tracked at 1 point at BSP from 15 Oct 2026 and judged after 150 bets each. Not a betting recommendation. First time on soft uses the going declared on the morning card; if the ground changes, the flag stays as declared.

How the figures work

Greyed "minor" notes under For / Against come from things that add nothing to a horse's chance once the rest of its profile is known (feature-strength check, Oct 2025–Oct 2026): change of going, course or trip, an excuse last time, first-time headgear, pace, draw and course run-style notes, jumping mistakes and falls, the length of a jumps break, carrying less weight, and "looks well handicapped" (all else equal, those horses do slightly worse). They are context, not reasons to back or oppose a horse.

Last 3: form fig / SR shows three past runs, oldest on the left and most recent on the right. Both numbers are in pounds on roughly the official-rating (OR) scale, so you can compare them with the OR column. –/– means there's no figure for that run, for example when the horse didn't finish or a margin is missing.

Form fig (bold) measures how well the horse ran against the field:

  1. Lengths beaten are converted to pounds at 15 ÷ furlongs: 3 lb a length at 5f, 2.5 lb at 6f, about 1.9 lb at 1m and about 0.9 lb at 2m. Margins are capped at 30 lengths.
  2. Weight: pounds carried (after the jockey's claim) above the race average are added back, and pounds below it are taken off.
  3. The race level is the median, across the rated runners, of OR + pounds beaten − weight adjustment, which is the winner's figure each runner's rating implies. At least 3 rated runners are needed; otherwise the provider's performance rating sets the level.
  4. Figure = race level − pounds beaten + weight adjustment. An ordinary handicap winner usually comes out close to its OR.

Jumps races: the form figure uses 30 ÷ furlongs pounds per length (about 1.9 lb at 2m and 1.25 lb at 3m), and the grey number is the provider's performance rating, because speed ratings aren't published for most jumps races. Falls, unseats, pulled-up runs and jumping mistakes mentioned in race comments come from the last six jumps runs.

SR (grey) is the Racing API's speed rating, worked out from the race time against the course-and-distance standard after removing the effect of the ground. The provider revises SRs after the race (non-handicap figures can change for up to 90 days).

Reading the two together: a form fig well above the SR means the horse finished close in a race that wasn't run quickly, so the form may flatter it if today's race looks strongly run. An SR well above the form fig means it ran fast despite an ordinary placing. Both rising across the three runs means the horse is improving.

Time vs std (in the expanded runner view) is our own time figure: pounds faster or slower than the median winning time at that course and distance, adjusted for the ground at that meeting. It's usually negative because it is measured against winners, so compare it between horses.

Run style is the horse's average position at the 2f marker over its last three RaceIQ runs: Leader, Prominent, Midfield or Held up (Unknown with fewer than 2 runs). Race shape counts the likely leaders: none means it could be slow and tactical, one is a lone leader, two is an even pace, and three or more is a strong pace.

Limits: the figures use the last 12 months of results only. Races with few rated runners can produce odd form figures. When the two figures disagree sharply, look closer before reading anything into it.

Backtests on six years of races found that none of these factors, alone or combined, beats Betfair SP. Use the reports to understand races and to spot where the model and the market disagree. They are not a list of value bets. Prices captured the evening before are early and incomplete, and Rule 4 is not applied.
